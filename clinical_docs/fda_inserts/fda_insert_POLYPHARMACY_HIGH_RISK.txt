PART I – DEFINING POLYPHARMACY IN HIGH-RISK PATIENTS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DEFINITION:
  Polypharmacy: concurrent use of ≥ 5 medications.
  Excessive polypharmacy: concurrent use of ≥ 10 medications.
  High-risk polypharmacy: concurrent use of medications with narrow therapeutic
  index, complex interactions, or significant risks in elderly or renally-
  impaired patients.

PREVALENCE:
  - ~42% of adults ≥ 65 years take ≥ 5 prescription medications
  - ~12% take ≥ 10 medications
  - Polypharmacy is the #1 preventable cause of adverse drug events (ADE) in
    elderly hospitalized patients

CLINICAL CONSEQUENCES:
  - Drug-drug interactions (DDI)
  - Drug-disease interactions (especially renal and hepatic impairment)
  - Adverse drug reactions (ADR): falls, bleeding, cognitive impairment, AKI
  - Non-adherence and pill burden fatigue
  - Increased hospitalizations and 30-day readmission rates
  - Estimated cost of medication-related morbidity: > $528 billion/year (US)

PART III – DRUG-DRUG INTERACTION ANALYSIS
───────────────────────────────────────────
Interaction 1: Aspirin + Clopidogrel (DAPT)
  Risk Level  : MODERATE (clinically necessary, managed)
  Interaction : Additive antiplatelet effect → increased bleeding risk (GI, CNS)
  FDA Note    : DAPT is standard of care for 12 months post-DES implantation
  Monitoring  : Stool guaiac testing; observe for signs of bleeding; PPI
                co-prescription recommended (omeprazole 20 mg daily)
  Copilot Flag: Verify PPI was prescribed at discharge; if absent → FLAG

Interaction 2: Lisinopril + Furosemide
  Risk Level  : MODERATE (clinically managed)
  Interaction : ACEi + loop diuretic may cause hypotension (first-dose effect),
                and additive hyperkalemia risk in CKD patients
  Patient Risk: CKD Stage 3 (eGFR 42) + K+ 5.6 mEq/L at admission → HIGH
  Monitoring  : BMP every 2–4 weeks after initiation; target K+ < 5.5 mEq/L

Interaction 3: Carvedilol + Insulin Glargine
  Risk Level  : MODERATE
  Interaction : Beta-blockers can mask tachycardia (the primary early warning
                sign of hypoglycemia); bradycardia and hypoglycemia may co-occur
  Monitoring  : Educate patient on alternative hypoglycemia symptoms (sweating,
                pallor); more frequent glucose monitoring; adjust insulin dose
                cautiously

Interaction 4: Atorvastatin 80 mg + Amlodipine 10 mg
  Risk Level  : LOW-MODERATE
  Interaction : Amlodipine increases atorvastatin plasma concentrations by ~18%
                via CYP3A4 inhibition. Risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis
                increased at high statin doses.
  FDA Label   : Atorvastatin 80 mg + amlodipine – no dose adjustment required,
                but monitor for unexplained muscle pain, weakness, or dark urine
  Monitoring  : CK level if myopathy symptoms; LFTs at baseline and 12 weeks

Interaction 5: Carvedilol + Albuterol (PRN)
  Risk Level  : MODERATE-HIGH (clinically concerning)
  Interaction : Non-selective beta-blocker (carvedilol) antagonizes
                beta-2-agonist (albuterol) bronchodilatory effect – may worsen
                bronchospasm in COPD patients
  Clinical    : Patient has GOLD Stage III COPD. Carvedilol chosen for HFrEF
                (EF 38%) – benefit outweighs risk but close monitoring required
  Alternative : Consider switching to cardioselective beta-1 blocker
                (bisoprolol or metoprolol succinate) which have less beta-2
                antagonism, IF cardiac status permits
  Monitoring  : Monitor SpO2, FEV1, and patient-reported dyspnea at each visit

Interaction 6: Furosemide in CKD Stage 3
  Risk Level  : MODERATE
  Interaction : Loop diuretics reduce intravascular volume; in CKD, excessive
                diuresis causes AKI via prerenal azotemia
  Patient Risk: Baseline creatinine 2.1–2.4 (already elevated); dehydration
                sensitivity HIGH
  Monitoring  : Daily weight; fluid intake-output; BMP every 2 weeks;
                hold if creatinine rises > 0.5 mg/dL from baseline
