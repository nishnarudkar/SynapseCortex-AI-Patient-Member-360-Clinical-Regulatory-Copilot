SECTION 1 – INDICATIONS AND USAGE
──────────────────────────────────
Metformin hydrochloride is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to
improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients ≥ 10 years of age
with type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Metformin is NOT indicated for treatment of type 1 diabetes mellitus or for
patients with diabetic ketoacidosis.

SECTION 2 – DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION
───────────────────────────────────────
Starting Dose (Adults):
  Immediate-release : 500 mg twice daily OR 850 mg once daily with meals.
  Extended-release  : 500–1000 mg once daily with the evening meal.

Maximum Dose:
  Immediate-release : 2550 mg/day in divided doses.
  Extended-release  : 2000–2500 mg/day depending on formulation.

Titration:
  Increase dose in increments of 500 mg weekly or 850 mg every 2 weeks as
  tolerated to reduce GI adverse effects.

Renal Impairment Dosing:
  eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m²     : No dose adjustment required.
  eGFR 45–59 mL/min/1.73 m²    : Use with caution; benefit-risk assessment
                                   required; monitor renal function every 3–6 months.
  eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m²    : [⚠️ CAUTION] Use not recommended; if prescribed,
                                   closely monitor renal function, withhold if
                                   eGFR falls below 30.
  eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²     : [❌ CONTRAINDICATED] Do not initiate or continue.

SECTION 3 – DOSAGE FORMS AND STRENGTHS
────────────────────────────────────────
  Tablets (immediate-release) : 500 mg, 850 mg, 1000 mg
  Tablets (extended-release)  : 500 mg, 750 mg, 1000 mg
  Oral Solution               : 500 mg/5 mL

SECTION 4 – CONTRAINDICATIONS
───────────────────────────────
Metformin is CONTRAINDICATED in patients with:

1.  RENAL IMPAIRMENT
    Estimated GFR (eGFR) < 30 mL/min/1.73 m².
    Rationale: Metformin is renally excreted almost exclusively unchanged.
    Renal impairment reduces clearance, leading to accumulation and markedly
    increased risk of lactic acidosis.

2.  ACUTE OR CHRONIC METABOLIC ACIDOSIS, including diabetic ketoacidosis.

3.  KNOWN HYPERSENSITIVITY to metformin hydrochloride or any component of the
    formulation (rare; manifests as urticaria, angioedema).

4.  RADIOLOGIC CONTRAST PROCEDURES (TEMPORARY):
    Withhold metformin at the time of or prior to iodinated contrast procedures
    in patients with eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²; reassess renal function 48 hours
    after procedure before restarting.

SECTION 5 – WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
──────────────────────────────────────
5.1  LACTIC ACIDOSIS (Black Box Warning)
     ─────────────────────────────────────
     ⚠️  BLACK BOX WARNING: LACTIC ACIDOSIS

     Post-marketing cases of metformin-associated lactic acidosis have resulted
     in death, hypothermia, hypotension, and resistant bradyarrhythmias.
     Onset is often subtle, with non-specific symptoms such as malaise, myalgia,
     respiratory distress, somnolence, and abdominal pain.

     Risk factors include:
       - Renal impairment (most significant risk factor)
       - Hepatic impairment
       - Congestive heart failure requiring pharmacologic intervention
       - Concomitant use of carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (topiramate, zonisamide,
         acetazolamide) – increases risk of metabolic acidosis
       - Excessive alcohol intake
       - Advanced age (particularly ≥ 65 years)
       - Dehydration or sepsis
       - Radiologic contrast studies

     ACTION IF SUSPECTED:
       Discontinue metformin immediately. Hospitalize patient. Institute supportive
       measures. Metformin and lactate can be removed by hemodialysis.

     HEDIS / CLINICAL COPILOT ALERT TRIGGER:
       Flag for pharmacovigilance review when:
         - Active metformin order + eGFR < 45 (HIGH RISK)
         - Active metformin order + eGFR < 30 (CONTRAINDICATED – ALERT REQUIRED)

5.2  RENAL IMPAIRMENT
     Assess eGFR before initiating metformin and at least annually thereafter.
     In patients at increased risk of renal impairment (elderly, those with
     cardiovascular disease, or concomitant nephrotoxic drugs), assess eGFR
     more frequently (every 3–6 months).

5.3  HEPATIC IMPAIRMENT
     Avoid metformin in patients with clinical or laboratory evidence of hepatic
     disease. Impaired lactate metabolism increases risk of lactic acidosis.

5.4  VITAMIN B12 DEFICIENCY
     Long-term use of metformin is associated with a reduction in vitamin B12
     absorption. Annual measurement of hematologic parameters is advised; check
     B12 levels if anemia or peripheral neuropathy develops.

5.5  HYPOGLYCEMIA
     Metformin alone rarely causes symptomatic hypoglycemia. Risk increases when
     combined with insulin secretagogues (sulfonylureas, meglitinides) or insulin.

SECTION 6 – ADVERSE REACTIONS
───────────────────────────────
Most Common (≥ 5%):
  Gastrointestinal: diarrhea (53%), nausea/vomiting (26%), flatulence (12%),
  abdominal discomfort (6%), indigestion (7%), asthenia (9%)
  (GI effects are dose-related and typically resolve with slower titration)

Serious (Postmarketing):
  - Lactic acidosis (see Black Box Warning)
  - Acute pancreatitis (rare)
  - Megaloblastic anemia (due to B12 deficiency with prolonged use)

SECTION 7 – DRUG INTERACTIONS
───────────────────────────────
7.1  Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors
     Topiramate, zonisamide, acetazolamide, and dichlorphenamide may reduce serum
     bicarbonate and increase risk of metformin-associated lactic acidosis.
     Use with caution; consider more frequent metabolic monitoring.

7.2  Alcohol
     Alcohol potentiates the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism.
     Advise patients to avoid excessive alcohol intake.

7.3  Iodinated Contrast Media
     Intravascular administration of iodinated contrast can lead to contrast-
     induced acute kidney injury, which may impair metformin clearance. Withhold
     metformin before and 48 hours after the procedure.

7.4  Drugs that Reduce Renal Function or Compete for Tubular Secretion
     Cationic drugs (cimetidine, amiloride, digoxin, morphine, procainamide,
     quinidine, quinine, ranitidine, triamterene, trimethoprim, vancomycin)
     compete with metformin for tubular secretion and may increase metformin
     plasma concentrations.

7.5  Insulin / Insulin Secretagogues
     Concomitant use with insulin or sulfonylureas increases hypoglycemia risk.
     Lower doses of the concomitant agent may be required.

SECTION 8 – USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
─────────────────────────────────────────
8.1  Pregnancy
     Limited data available. Poorly controlled diabetes in pregnancy carries
     significant risks. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed; discuss
     risk-benefit with patient.

8.2  Lactation
     Small amounts present in breast milk. Consider developmental benefits of
     breastfeeding and risks to the infant.

8.4  Pediatric Use
     Safety and effectiveness established in pediatric patients ≥ 10 years with
     type 2 diabetes (immediate-release only). Extended-release: safety in
     pediatrics not established.

8.5  Geriatric Use
     Age-related decline in renal function may increase risk of lactic acidosis.
     Monitor renal function more frequently. Titrate conservatively. Do not
     initiate in patients > 80 years unless renal function confirms eGFR ≥ 45.

SECTION 10 – OVERDOSAGE
─────────────────────────
Overdose of metformin has led to lactic acidosis (see Section 5.1). Metformin
is dialyzable with a clearance up to 170 mL/min under good hemodynamic
conditions. Prompt hemodialysis is recommended for management of overdose.

SECTION 11 – DESCRIPTION
──────────────────────────
Metformin hydrochloride is a white to off-white crystalline compound with the
empirical formula C4H11N5·HCl and a molecular weight of 165.63. It is freely
soluble in water, slightly soluble in ethanol, and practically insoluble in
acetone, ether, and chloroform.

SECTION 12 – CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
─────────────────────────────────────
12.1 Mechanism of Action
     Metformin decreases hepatic glucose production (gluconeogenesis and
     glycogenolysis), decreases intestinal absorption of glucose, and improves
     insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake and utilization.

12.3 Pharmacokinetics
     Bioavailability (fasting) : 50–60%
     Tmax                      : 2.5 hours (IR)
     Protein binding           : Negligible
     Metabolism                : Not metabolized; excreted unchanged
     Elimination               : Renal tubular secretion (primary route)
     Half-life (plasma)        : ~6.2 hours
     Half-life (blood)         : ~17.6 hours
     Renal clearance           : ~3.5× greater than creatinine clearance
     CYP interactions          : None (not a CYP substrate/inhibitor/inducer)
